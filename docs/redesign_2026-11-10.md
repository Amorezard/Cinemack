# Cinemack five-page redesign (Tue 10 Nov 2026, round 17)

Brief: "Complete website redesign & implementation master prompt" (Ask Cinemack, Pick Tonight, Release Calendar,
Watchlist, Profile) plus six approved reference images. Everything ships at `/v8` (the classic page stays at `/`).
Nothing in the answer pipeline, retrieval, the v8 evaluation scripts or the sealed sets changed.

## 3. What each page does now
- **Ask Cinemack**: collapsible conversation sidebar (New chat, search, grouped Today / Yesterday / Previous 7 days /
  30 days / Older, rename, delete with confirmation); phones get a drawer. Recommendation cards: poster, title, year,
  genre, runtime, rating with its source (IMDb or TMDB), the personal reason when retrieval recorded one, Watchlist,
  Where to watch, Showtimes (only for films on TMDB's now-playing list), Details. Sources as a "Sources (n)"
  disclosure with the CC BY-SA and TMDB credits. Follow-up chips, thumbs, copy, and the answer time. Filter chips
  above the composer (Genre, Length, Era, My services) share `S.filters` with Home. The chat column scrolls; the page
  does not.
- **Pick Tonight**: five dropdown chips (who, mood, time, familiar/different, services) and Find My Picks. Up to three
  ranked cards; #1 is larger with a yellow border and a Best Match badge. Reasons come only from the film's facts and
  the person's choices (mood genre, runtime against the limit, rating against "who is watching", on your services,
  rating), plus the retrieval's personal reason and the model's one line on that film. Hard limits are sent with
  `strict: true` and checked again in the page; a film with an unknown runtime or rating fails. Fewer than three: it
  says how many, lists what was left out and why, and offers specific buttons to loosen a limit (it never loosens one
  by itself). Try different picks (uses unseen films from the same search first), Refine, Save all, Ask about these
  (opens that conversation in Ask Cinemack).
- **Calendar**: Week / Month toggle, type filters (All, In Theaters, Streaming, TV, My Watchlist), service chips built
  from the services that actually appear, previous / next / Today. Week columns have a fixed height: at most four
  posters and an exact "+N more". Month is a real grid with type dots, counts and two posters per cell. The selected
  day opens grouped In Theaters / Streaming / TV cards (beside the grid on wide screens). Date, view, filter and service
  live in the URL, so switching views keeps them. "US release, type not confirmed" and "Streaming service not
  announced yet" are shown as such.
- **Watchlist**: Overview, Movies, TV Shows, Coming Soon, Released (Shared only when a friend shares a list). Up Next
  is a real saved film (the server's watch-next order) with backdrop, facts, availability and actions; a poster strip
  beside it; Coming From Your Watchlist with per-title alert bells; Continue Watching; shelves that never repeat a film
  (on your services, in theaters, streaming elsewhere, coming soon, recently added); All movies with filters and sort.
  Empty list: onboarding cards (search and add, Pick Tonight, calendar, import).
- **Profile**: banner (backdrop of the person's top-rated or most recent film, otherwise default artwork; no photo is
  ever invented), initial avatar, name, Edit profile, Share friend code (accounts only; there is no public profile),
  six real statistics. Tabs Overview / Ratings / Reviews / Watch History / Friends, plus Settings with its own
  navigation (Profile and appearance, Streaming services, Preferences, Notifications, Privacy, Imports and connections,
  Account and security; account deletion separated and confirmed by password + typing DELETE). Overview: Continue
  Watching (same data as Watchlist), Recent Activity (view all), Your Taste (inferred genre shares, labelled with how
  many films they rest on and "early signal" under five; chosen preferences shown apart), Favorite Films (highest
  rated, or thumbs-up, labelled which).

## 6. Security and privacy findings
- **HTTPS: not in place (blocker for public registration).** The site is plain HTTP on cs.merrimack.edu:49151;
  passwords and the session cookie cross the network unencrypted and the cookie cannot be `Secure`. Ports 80/443 on the
  machine belong to another service and nginx/caddy/certbot are not installed for this account (round 16 check).
  Recommendation: keep the site behind the access code and do not advertise sign-up until the department puts it behind
  an HTTPS proxy (or issues a certificate for a port we can use). The sign-up form and Account settings say this plainly.
- Passwords: scrypt (N=2^17, r=8, p=1) because argon2-cffi is not installed on the server; existing accounts are not
  migrated (upgraded to Argon2id automatically on next sign-in if the package is installed later). Sessions: random
  token, SHA-256 stored, HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, 30 days. Login failures are rate limited (`login_fail`).
- Privacy: no new route takes another person's key. Friends' data still goes only through `/api/v8/friend/{key}`
  (friendship + per-category check). Account deletion now also removes TV progress and alert opt-outs and needs the
  password plus typing DELETE.
- No credentials are logged by the new code; errors shown in the page are the server's own messages.

## 7. Not done / limits (honest list)
- Episode-level data: facts.db has season premiere dates only, no episode lists, counts or air dates. So no episode
  percentages, no "next episode airs" dates, and "next episode" is the next number after the one the person recorded.
- "Resume" cannot open a player: no streaming service integration exists. Cards offer "Watched S2 E5" and Update.
- Watch history comes only from Netflix / Letterboxd files, Trakt, and "Mark watched"; Cinemack never sees playback.
- Alerts appear in the bell only (no email or push), as before.
- Banner: chosen from the person's own films; uploading a custom banner is not built (no image storage).
- Showtimes are web searches (Google, Fandango, AMC, Regal, Showcase); no authorised showtime API.
- Chat latency was not re-profiled in this round: the shared A10 is still held by another user's `vasp_std` job, which
  makes numbers measure that job (round 14b). Streaming, early film cards and short answers are unchanged.
  Round 17 starts a small benchmark in tmux `bench` for the record.
- Screenshots use fixture data and flat colour tiles for posters (the work container cannot reach TMDB); the live check
  is round 17's smoke test plus opening /v8 on a phone.
- `/` still serves the classic page. Switching `/` to v8 is the user's decision.
- Lighthouse itself was not run; numbers are from the browser's performance entries.

## 8. Rollback
`archive/pre_r17/` holds the replaced files. To roll back:
`cp -rp archive/pre_r17/v8/* web/v8/ && cp -p archive/pre_r17/*.py web/ && rm -f web/v8extra.py web/dbsafe.py web/v8/{core,home,film,chat,pick,calendar,watchlist,profile}.js`
then restart the site (tmux `site`). The new tables are left in place and are harmless to the old code.
Commits are split (connection fix, data routes, page, tools and docs) so one can be reverted alone.
