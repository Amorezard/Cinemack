About Cinemack

Cinemack (until November 2026 called Movie Buff) is a student research project at Merrimack College: a film chatbot that answers from a database of about 32,000 films and checks its answers against it. It is not a commercial product.

Where the data comes from

TMDB

This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.

Film details, cast and crew, posters, backdrops and cast photos come from The Movie Database (TMDB). Images load straight from TMDB's servers; Cinemack does not keep copies.

Wikipedia

Plot, production, reception and box-office passages come from English Wikipedia, used under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence. Answers that use Wikipedia text link the articles they used.

Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic and CinemaScore figures on film cards are the ones stated in each film's Wikipedia article, so they can be out of date.

IMDb

Information courtesy of IMDb (https://www.imdb.com). Used with permission. IMDb ratings come from IMDb's non-commercial datasets.

Wikidata

Awards and some budget and box-office figures come from Wikidata (CC0).

JustWatch

Where-to-watch listings (streaming, rental and purchase) come from JustWatch, through TMDB.

Reviews

Professional reviews: The Guardian (powered by the Guardian Open Platform): the star rating and one-line summary, linked to the full review. The New York Times (data provided by The New York Times): only who reviewed a film, when, and whether it was a Critic's Pick, with a link; no New York Times text is ever given to Cinemack's language models.

Critic statements in the Reception panel and in answers come from each film's Wikipedia "Reception" section, with the outlet and critic that Wikipedia cites, linked to the original review where Wikipedia gives a link.

Rotten Tomatoes and Metacritic scores for films whose Wikipedia article gives none come from OMDb.

YouTube

Trailers play from YouTube in privacy-enhanced mode, only when you press play. Trailer view counts come from the YouTube Data API and are today's counts.

Video essays and analysis come from a short list of film channels, using only each video's title, description and chapter list, always linked. Stored YouTube data is refreshed or deleted within 30 days.

MovieLens & Netflix

Audience ratings and tags from the MovieLens datasets (GroupLens, University of Minnesota). Netflix viewing figures from Netflix's published "What We Watched" engagement reports.

What gets saved

  • Your questions and Cinemack's answers are logged to improve the system.
  • The buttons on film cards are saved for this browser. "Liked it" and "Not for me" shape its recommendations; "Haven't seen" and "Want to watch" are kept as a list. "Was this answer helpful?" rates the answer only, never the film.
  • Without an account, everything is tied to an anonymous id stored in your browser. With one, it follows your account. Passwords are stored only as a salted hash.
  • Memory: short notes from things you tell Cinemack about yourself (who you watch with, what you can't stand) are kept and used in later answers. You can see, delete or turn off memory under your name or "Sign in" at the top, and a private chat is neither saved nor remembered.
  • Deleting your account removes your chats, film choices, settings and memory.
