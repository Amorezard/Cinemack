How Movie Buff finds your film

Movie Buff is a language model that answers from a database of real film records instead of from memory. That's what lets it find a film from a vague description, and say plainly when it doesn't know.

Your question→Understand→Search ×3→Read→Answer→Check

What happens when you ask

Every question goes through the same steps. Switch on Details in the chat to watch them for any answer.

  1. Read the question understanding

    The model works out what you're asking: a film you named, a plot you're describing, or a recommendation. For a description it also writes a short guess at how a film database would summarise that plot.

  2. Search three ways hybrid retrieval

    It searches about 30,000 films by meaning (vector search), by exact words like names and places (keyword search), and by its guessed plot, then merges the three rankings into one.

  3. Read the candidates re-ranking

    For descriptions, the model reads the plots of the top 50 candidates and picks the ones that fit, taking a second look further down the list if nothing does. A year you mention counts as a hint, because people often misremember them.

  4. Answer from the records grounding

    The chosen films' records (year, director, cast, rating, plot) go to the model with instructions to use only those facts. If nothing matched, it says so instead of guessing.

  5. Check the answer fact check

    Every name, year, rating and film in the finished answer is checked against the records it was given, and the result is shown under the answer.

  6. You rate it feedback loop

    Thumbs up or down go into a log, and every thumbs-down becomes a candidate test question for the next round of improvements.

How well it works

Measured on questions set aside for testing, including real "what's that movie?" posts from the TREC Tip-of-the-Tongue benchmark.

Strengths and limits

What to expect before you ask.

Good at

  • Finding a film from a half-remembered plot, even with the details slightly wrong
  • Directors, main cast, release year and TMDB rating for about 30,000 films
  • Saying "I don't have that film" instead of inventing one
  • Follow-ups like "who directed it?" or "what about the sequel?"

Not so good at

  • Very obscure films: the database keeps films with at least 50 ratings on TMDB, plus the most popular recent releases
  • Endings and twists: the stored plot summaries stop before the ending
  • Small roles: only the top five cast members are stored
  • Speed: a long description can take 10–20 seconds, and questions are answered one at a time on a shared GPU
